alternation, stride period, posture smoothness, energy-per-distance. Do
NOT duplicate the base reward (forward velocity, the healthy bonus or
torque cost).

STRONG PREFERENCE: write the aux_reward as a BONUS (positive values when
the policy is doing the right thing), not a PENALTY (negative values
scaled by the degree of a failure). A pure penalty like
``-float(np.square(obs[10]))`` creates a local optimum where the policy
can minimise the penalty by not moving --- we have measured this failure
mode on Walker2d-v5 TD3 under this exact setup. A bounded bonus like
``float(np.exp(-np.square(obs[10])))`` (which is 1 when the pitch rate
is zero and decays smoothly as it grows) has the same shaping gradient
at small values of the quantity being shaped, but is never worse than
returning zero, so it cannot create a stand-still trap. When in doubt,
convert ``-f(x)`` into ``np.exp(-f(x))`` or a clipped
``max(0.0, 1.0 - f(x))`` form.

AUX INGREDIENT GUIDANCE (Walker2d-v5)

Avoid:
- Anything that rewards a LOW torso: obs[0] is the height, and below 0.8
  the episode ends. Never use a form like exp(-|obs[0]|).
- Any penalty-form (``-f(x)``) — see stand-still trap note above.
- Rewarding standing still: the healthy bonus already pays for staying
  up, so a bonus that peaks at zero velocity teaches the walker to stand.

Prefer these ingredient families instead:
- Forward progress Φ(s), e.g.
    def aux_reward(obs, action, info):
        v = float(info["x_velocity"])
        return float(np.tanh(v / 4.0))
  — a saturating forward-velocity bonus, bounded in [-1, 1]. Under
  centering + PBRS wrapping this becomes γ·Φ(s')−Φ(s) which is a credit-
  assignment signal aligned with the base reward (Ng, Harada, Russell
  1999 Theorem 1: this preserves the optimal policy).
- Upright-torso bonus: ``np.exp(-np.square(obs[1]))`` peaks with the torso
  level, which keeps the walker away from the pitch limit that ends the
  episode, while the base reward still pays for moving forward.
- Stride-rhythm bonus: reward out-of-phase right/left thigh angles, e.g.
  ``np.sin(obs[2] - obs[5])`` or ``np.abs(obs[2] - obs[5])``, which encodes
  the alternating leg phase of walking.
